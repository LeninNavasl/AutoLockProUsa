An ignition switch lock cylinder rarely fails without warning. Before it locks up permanently—leaving you stranded with an engine that won't start and a steering wheel that won't unlock—it exhibits distinct mechanical symptoms.

Recognizing these symptoms early allows you to have a mobile automotive locksmith service or re-key the cylinder in your own driveway before a catastrophic breakdown occurs.

## 1. Key Resists Turning or Needs "Jiggling"

The most frequent early warning sign is needing to wiggle, pull outward slightly, or jiggle the key to turn it past the `ACC` (Accessory) or `ON` positions.

- **The Cause:** Inside the lock cylinder, a sequence of split brass wafers must align perfectly with the key blade grooves. As key edges round off and tumbler springs weaken, the wafers catch against the cylinder sidewall.
- **The Danger:** Forcing the key harder will eventually twist or snap the metal blade off inside the tumbler housing.

## 2. Key Sticks When Trying to Shut Down or Extract

If turning the car off requires noticeable effort, or if the key refuses to slide smoothly out of the cylinder when parked in `P`, the retaining detent or wafer springs have become fouled by metal burrs or dried factory grease.

Never spray standard WD-40 or silicone lubricant into an automotive ignition cylinder; standard petroleum distillates attract airborne grit and create an abrasive paste that accelerates tumbler wear.

## 3. Intermittent Accessory Power Loss

When an internal tumbler or switch contacts wear down, electrical continuity to the dashboard and accessory circuits can cut out while driving over potholes or rough pavement. If your radio or dashboard lights flicker when you lightly touch the key head, the electrical switch behind the cylinder needs replacement.

## 4. How Mobile Locksmiths Save You Dealership Rebuild Costs

Dealerships typically replace the entire steering column lock assembly and hand you a new key that no longer matches your exterior car doors—forcing you to carry two separate keys.

A certified mobile locksmith rebuilds and re-keys your ignition cylinder on-site, matching the replacement brass wafers precisely to your existing door key so that one single key continues to operate the entire vehicle.
